## Literature Review: Research Comparison Chart

This table compares five research articles examining
the impact of generative AI in higher education,
including student experiences, learning outcomes,
critical thinking, and academic performance.

| Article | Focus | Method | Findings | Connections |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **"ChatGPT Seems Too Good to Be True" (2024)** | Student use and perceptions of ChatGPT | Survey of 1,001 college students | AI use varies by student background and university policies. Students raised concerns about ethics, answer quality, and learning. | Helps explain why students use AI and what concerns they have. |
| **One Year in the Classroom with ChatGPT (2025)** | AI-supported classroom learning | Classroom activities and surveys of 169 students | Students generally found ChatGPT helpful and engaging but did not see it as a replacement for instructors. | Shows how students experience AI in actual courses. |
| **ChatGPT Effects on Cognitive Skills (2024)** | Critical, creative, and reflective thinking | Experimental study with 125 students | Researchers reported improvements in critical-thinking scores among students who used ChatGPT. | Helps investigate how AI use relates to students' thinking skills. |
| **ChatGPT Improves Creative Problem-Solving (2024)** | Creativity and problem-solving | Experimental study comparing 77 AI users with 68 students without AI | Students using ChatGPT produced higher-quality and more original solutions, but some may have overestimated their work. | Explores AI's benefits and the importance of evaluating AI-assisted work. |
| **Expanding the Lens (2026)** | Student AI use across two Spanish institutions | Multi-institutional study | Examines AI use, academic performance, and differences across courses. Exact findings need to be verified. | Adds evidence about AI use in real university settings across institutions. |
